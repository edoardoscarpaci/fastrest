# Regulatory posture — SBOMs, the EU Cyber Resilience Act, and NIS2

> ⚠️ **This is a position, not legal advice.** We are not lawyers. This document states what varco's
> maintainers believe applies to *this project*, why we believe it, and exactly which facts the
> belief depends on — so that you can check those facts yourself and reach your own conclusion. It
> makes **no claim of CRA compliance or certification**, and nothing here is an assessment of *your*
> obligations. If varco is part of a product you place on the EU market, your obligations are yours,
> and you should take advice on them.

Written for Plan 030 / Phase 3 (BACKLOG row **D5**). Evidence:

## What we actually ship

Every release publishes, **per distribution** (ten of them), a **CycloneDX 1.6** SBOM in JSON:

| Where | How | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **GitHub Release assets** | `sboms/<name>.cdx.json`, attached by `release.yml`'s `release-assets` job | ⭐ **The canonical location** |
| **Inside each wheel** | `.dist-info/sboms/<name>.cdx.json`, per **PEP 770** | For consumers who only ever `pip install` |

Generated by `scripts/sbom.py` from `uv.lock` — `uv export --package <name> --no-dev` piped into a
version-pinned `cyclonedx-bom`. Reproducible: two runs over one lockfile produce byte-identical
documents, so a consumer diffing two releases sees only real changes.

**One SBOM per distribution, not one workspace-wide document.** `varco-core` has ~25 components; the
whole workspace has ~154. A workspace-wide document attached to `varco-core` would over-report by
roughly 6×, and an over-reporting SBOM is *actively misleading* to the regulated downstream it
exists to serve — it would match vulnerabilities against components that consumer never installs.

⚠️ **PyPI does not yet serve PEP 770 SBOMs** as a separate artifact or metadata endpoint (research
004 §3). A consumer who wants them without downloading and unzipping ten wheels should take them
from the GitHub Release. That is why both pathways exist.

## Why we publish them at all — credibility, not obligation

An earlier draft of this work carried the rationale *"an obligation, not a feature"* and cited "EU
CRA reporting obligations bind from September 2026". **Both are wrong, and the correction is the
point of this document.**

- Full CRA enforcement is **2027-12-11** (three years after the Regulation entered into force on
  2024-12-10), not September 2026.
- More importantly, we do not believe the CRA's manufacturer obligations bind varco *at all* — see
  below.

We publish SBOMs because **downstream consumers who *are* CRA- or NIS2-regulated genuinely need
them from their upstreams**, and because an accurate component list is useful to anyone doing
vulnerability management regardless of regulation. That is the honest case, and it is enough.

## The CRA position — the non-commercial FOSS exemption

**Position: varco falls under the CRA's non-commercial free-and-open-source-software exemption, and
is therefore not a "product with digital elements" placed on the market by a manufacturer.**

The reasoning, and the facts it rests on:

1. **The exemption covers FOSS not monetized by its developers.** varco is developed and released
   free of charge under Apache-2.0.
2. **We accept no donations.** The threshold in the guidance is donations *exceeding the costs* of
   design, development and provision — we take none at all, so the threshold is not approached.
3. **We sell no support, no SLA-backed hosting, and no professional services** around varco. Each of
   these would constitute commercial activity and would remove the exemption.
4. **We are not an "open-source software steward" either.** That category — lighter obligations, but
   real ones — covers entities supporting FOSS development *for commercial purposes* (a
   vendor-backed foundation, a corporate open-source programme office). varco has no such backer.

### ⚠️ What would change this

The position depends on **facts about how the project is funded**, not on a legal reading that stays
true regardless. It would need re-examination the day any of these becomes true:

- accepting donations that exceed the project's costs;
- offering paid support, consulting, or a commercial licence;
- offering SLA-backed hosting of varco or a varco-based service;
- a company adopting stewardship of the project for commercial purposes.

If you are reading this after such a change and this document still says the above, **the document
is stale — treat that as a bug and open an issue.**

## The NIS2 position

**NIS2 (Directive 2022/2555) does not bind FOSS authors or projects.** It places obligations on
*organizations that use* software — essential and important entities in the sectors it covers — to
assess and manage their own supply-chain risk.

So: NIS2 is not a varco concern, and it *is* a concern for a NIS2-regulated organization that uses
varco. If that is you, the SBOM above is the artifact your own supply-chain risk process most likely
wants from us. What we cannot do is discharge your obligation on your behalf.
